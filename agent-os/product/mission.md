# Product Mission

## Problem

Professional profiles on LinkedIn and generic resume sites fail to convey *how* someone thinks, decides, and solves problems. Traditional CVs and corporate landing pages reduce a technical career to a list of job titles and technology names, stripping away context, reasoning, and personality.

## Target Users

Primary audience: technical leaders and decision-makers who need to evaluate seniority, technical judgment, and architectural thinking — not keyword-matching recruiters.

- CTOs, VPs of Engineering, Heads of Engineering
- Software architects and solutions architects
- Technical leads and senior engineers

The site should be read and understood by people who know what distributed systems, enterprise integration, and technical leadership actually look like in practice.

## Solution

A personal site for **Pedro Bengoa** — Solutions Architect and technical leader with nearly 20 years of experience — that functions as a **personal brand + professional CV + technical portfolio**, without looking like any of them.

### Reinventing myself, and bringing others along

Over nearly 20 years, the way we build software has changed under my feet again and again. Each time, I've had to relearn my craft while also leading a team through the same change: coaching engineers, mentoring technical leads, and keeping delivery going while everything shifted.

Some of these transformations were bigger or more impactful than others:

- From server-rendered backend pages to rich UI applications
- From monoliths to microservices
- From stateful to stateless applications
- From SVN to Git
- From managed servers to cloud services
- From native Android and iOS apps to cross-platform mobile with React Native
- And, most recently, from manual coding to AI-driven development

The pattern repeats: learn it first, figure out what actually matters, turn it into something others can use, then help everyone on the team get there, not just the early adopters. Reinventing myself is part of the job. Doing it while bringing a team along is the part I care about most.

Supporting evidence for case studies (in `data/Personal_experience.md`): OpenShift 3→4 migration, Java 8→11 / `javax`→`jakarta`, reverse-engineering and documenting the client CI/CD pipeline, and AI/SDD adoption across three teams.

### Core concept

> Technology is a tool. The interesting part is figuring out what to build, why, and how.

The site communicates experience and seniority through content and evidence, not through grandiose claims. It shows not just *what* technologies are known and *what* roles were held, but *how* problems were approached, *why* decisions were made, and *what* was learned.

### Principles

- Professional, technical, personal, modern, clear, authentic
- Humor without self-importance; credibility without corporate clichés
- No exaggerated capabilities, no invented experience
- Content-first: evidence speaks louder than adjectives
- Bilingual (Spanish and English) with culturally adapted copy — not mechanical translation
- SEO-first from the start: semantic HTML, structured data, hreflang, Open Graph, sitemap, clean URLs

### Content architecture (flexible for growth)

Designed to expand into a **technical portfolio / knowledge hub** over time: technical notes, articles, experiments, architecture diagrams, case studies. Structure supports this without requiring a concept rethink.
